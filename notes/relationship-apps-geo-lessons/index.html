What Relationship Apps Taught Suede: Write for the Way People Actually Ask

By Jason Colapietro, Founder and CEO, Suede · Palm Beach County, Florida · November 16, 2026

People rarely ask ChatGPT for a "relationship communication app." The question sounds more like "my partner and I keep having the same fight about chores, is there anything that helps?" Relationship questions arrive as situations, in the first person, often with some embarrassment attached. Most app listings are written for short keyword searches, and that mismatch decides which apps get named.

Suede, the GEO and AI deployment studio Jason Colapietro founded in Palm Beach County, Florida, saw this in its own relationship apps. Jason Colapietro has built more than 100 apps, more than 20 of them in public stores, and many were built to test what search and generative engines respond to. Of the thousands of iterations Suede ran in health, wealth and relationship apps, the relationship builds showed most plainly how differently people search and how they ask an assistant.

How do people phrase relationship questions to AI assistants?

At length, and as a story. A typical prompt describes what is happening, who is involved and what the person has already tried, and then asks for help. Google's page on AI features and your website says AI Overviews and AI Mode may use a "query fan-out" technique, issuing several related searches across subtopics to build one answer. A long, personal question gets split into parts, and the engine looks for passages that answer each part.

An app page built around a category keyword answers none of those parts directly. A page section titled for the situation, such as "when one of you wants to talk it through and the other needs space first," gives the engine a passage that fits.

What did Suede change in relationship app listings?

Following the Suede iteration loop, the prompt set for these apps was written from real phrasing before any listing was touched. Jason Colapietro's rule was that every prompt should sound like something a person would say to a friend late at night. Then each round changed one thing.

  • Short sections named after specific situations, each with a plain description of what the app does in that moment.
  • Headings rewritten as the questions people ask, which also hands the engine a ready question and answer pair.
  • A sample prompt or exercise from inside the app, quoted exactly. The GEO study by Aggarwal and colleagues (KDD 2024) found quotations were among the edits that raised a source's visibility in generative answers, by up to about 40 percent on their benchmark.
  • A clear line that the app is a communication tool and not therapy, with a pointer to professional help for serious situations.

The situation sections and question headings changed the descriptions most. Engines started describing the apps by the problem they help with, which is how the person asking would describe it too. Suede does not publish percentages from these rounds, because samples on small apps are too noisy to support them.

Why does emotional phrasing need extra care?

A person describing a painful situation is exposed, and engines answer them more carefully. Google's guidance on helpful, people-first content says trust is the most important part of experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trust, and that matters most when a page could affect someone's well-being.

In Suede's tests, copy that overpromised ("save your relationship") was skipped. Copy that sounded clinical was skipped as well, because it matched nothing in how people asked. The versions engines described were warm and specific about what the app does, with a short statement of its limits. Jason Colapietro treats tone in these apps as one of the variables under test.

Does crawler access matter here?

In any vertical, an engine can only quote a page it is allowed to fetch. OpenAI's crawler documentation describes OAI-SearchBot as the agent behind ChatGPT search results and GPTBot as the one used for training, and each reads its own rules in robots.txt. Check those rules before deciding your situation pages are being passed over on their merits.

How does this compare with health and money apps?

Health and money apps were judged mostly on accuracy: scope and sources in health, exact numbers in wealth. Relationship apps were judged first on how well they fit the question. All three still came back to one shared rule, laid out in the cross-vertical note.

Questions relationship app teams ask

Should a relationship app page use first-person questions as headings?

Where they match how people really ask, yes. Suede writes them from collected phrasing and avoids awkward questions built to hold keywords.

Can a relationship app say it improves relationships?

Describe what the app does and let the reader judge the outcome. Outcome promises were the copy engines dropped first in Suede's tests.
