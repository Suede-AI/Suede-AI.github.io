From Book to Practice: How Suede AI Applies Stake Your Claim in Client SEO and GEO Work

By Jason Colapietro, Founder and CEO, Suede AI · West Palm Beach, Florida · November 14, 2026

A strategy book is only as good as the Tuesday morning it survives. Stake Your Claim was written for creatives, but Jason Colapietro and the Suede AI team use its five parts as the skeleton of how they run search visibility work for businesses in West Palm Beach, Jupiter and the rest of Palm Beach County.

This note walks through that mapping, part by part, and is specific about what happens in each stage and what it costs the client in time and attention.

Part I, The Window: start with a baseline, not a pitch

The first part of the book is about timing. The window to establish yourself during a structural shift does not stay open, and the people who measure the shift early get to act on it first.

In client work, that becomes a baseline. Before anything changes, Suede AI records what the major assistants and search engines say about the business and its category today. Which competitors do ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google's AI results name for "best accountant in Palm Beach Gardens"? Is the business described correctly, incorrectly or not at all? That snapshot becomes the reference point for every later decision, and it is the core of the Suede AI assessment.

Part II, The Instruments: fix the machinery first

Part II covers the systems and rails that let work compound without constant manual effort. On a website, those instruments are technical: clean crawl paths, fast pages, correct canonical tags, a sitemap that matches reality, and structured data that describes the business as one consistent entity.

Suede AI handles this under technical SEO. The detail that matters most for AI search is the entity graph. Every page on a client site should point to the same organization and person identifiers rather than minting new ones. Suede practices this on its own network: this book site, the founder page, the agency site and every satellite reference one canonical record for Jason Colapietro and one for the company. A model that sees the same identifiers everywhere builds one confident picture instead of several weak ones.

Instruments also include machine-readable summaries such as llms.txt, which give AI crawlers a short, plain-text map of what the site is and which pages matter. They take an afternoon to write and they remove guesswork.

Part III, The Craft: write pages that answer

The craft section of the book separates people who build from people who watch. In search work, craft is writing. Assistants quote pages that answer a specific question in the first two sentences and then support the answer with detail. They skip pages that open with a mission statement.

So the Suede team writes answer pages: one question per page, phrased the way a customer in Jupiter or Boca Raton would phrase it, with a direct answer, specifics, and a clear next step. Proof gets linked inline rather than asserted. When Suede AI cites its own standing, it uses checkable facts, such as Jason Colapietro's 54 merged pull requests across 46 open-source repositories he does not maintain, with the receipt attached. Suede AI ranks among Clutch's Top 30 AI Consultants in Florida and Top 50 AI Consultants nationwide (November 2026). The evidence ledger is where all of those receipts live.

Part IV, The Blueprints: one roadmap per business type

Part IV of the book gives separate roadmaps for musicians, visual artists, writers and podcasters, because the same principles play out differently in each craft. Client work follows the same logic. A roofing contractor, a surf shop and a dental practice need different answer pages, different proof and different local signals.

Suede AI has built concept sites to work out those blueprints in public. The Palm and Peak Roofing concept notes show what a Palm Beach County contractor build looks like, and the Blueline Surf concept notes show the same thinking applied to a Jupiter surf shop. They are concepts, not client case studies, and they are labeled that way. Their value is that anyone can inspect the structure.

Part V, What Endures: the client keeps the asset

The final part of the book is about ownership and inheritance: build things that stay yours. In an SEO and GEO engagement, that principle means the assets the work produces should belong to the client. The domain, the content, the structured data, the proof page and the measurement history are built to stay with the business, not to sit inside a platform it cannot take elsewhere.

This is also where the tradeoffs live. Owned visibility is slower to build than paid traffic. AI answers shift on their own schedule, so progress is measured in weeks and months. And no honest agency can promise a specific placement in a model's answer. What Suede AI can commit to is a clear, documented, verifiable presence that compounds over time.
