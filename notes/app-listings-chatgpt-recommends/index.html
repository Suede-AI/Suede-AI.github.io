What an Assistant Reads Before It Recommends Your App: Anatomy of a Listing

By Jason Colapietro, Founder and CEO, Suede · Jupiter, Florida · November 16, 2026

Type "what's a good app for practicing singing?" into ChatGPT and you get a short list of names. The assistant behind that list has usually never installed any of them. Its answer is assembled from text: the public store listing, the developer's own website, reviews, articles and forum threads that mention the app by name. The parts of a listing that an assistant can read are a smaller set than the parts a store's own search uses, and the support page you control is often the clearest source it finds.

Suede, the company Jason Colapietro founded in 2024 in Palm Beach County, Florida (suedeai.ai), builds and lists apps of its own, so this question is a practical one there. Suede Sing, an online singing-practice app at sing.suedeai.ai, went from at most 5 users to more than 1,000 active users within about a week of ChatGPT naming it, by Jason Colapietro's account. In Google Play's acquisition report for Suede Voice through November 5, 2026, ChatGPT accounted for 145 of 528 acquisition events. Neither figure says why an assistant picked those apps. Both are reasons to read a listing the way an assistant does.

Which fields can an assistant read?

Both stores publish a web version of every listing (apps.apple.com and play.google.com). That page is what a crawler sees, and it is the version to audit.

Privacy labels on the App Store and the data safety section on Google Play are also public text. For a health or relationship app, they answer a question an assistant is likely to be asked, which the note on GEO for health, wealth and relationship apps covers in more depth.

Character limits for the text fields

App Store: name 30 characters, subtitle 30, promotional text 170, description 4,000, keyword field 100. Google Play: title 30 characters, short description 80, full description 4,000. Both stores revise their guidelines from time to time, so confirm the current limits in App Store Connect or Play Console before rewriting.

Why does the developer name matter so much?

It is the field that ties an app to a company or a person across every other source. Jason Colapietro's iOS apps ship under his own Apple developer account, which means the developer page lists his name next to each app, and that name matches the byline on his writing and the founder record at suedeai.ai/founder. An assistant that reads the listing and the founder page sees the same person both times. If your developer name is a holding-company name nobody searches for, the listing loses that connection.

What should the support page say?

Apple requires a support URL, and Google Play asks for a developer website. Many developers point both at a contact form. A better use of that link is a short page on your domain that a stranger, or a model, can read in under a minute:

  • one sentence on what the app does and who it helps, matching the store subtitle
  • the platforms it runs on, with links to both store listings
  • pricing in plain words, including what is free
  • who makes it, with a link to the company page
  • a way to reach a person

You can change it in an afternoon, and the change goes live without waiting for store review.

How do you tell whether any of it is working?

Store dashboards show part of the picture. Google Play's acquisition report names some referrers, ChatGPT among them for Suede Voice, and lumps others together. The Suede Voice data note lays out that report, the Suede Sing case study tells the story of the week after ChatGPT named the app, and the field guide to measuring AI referrals covers where else to look. Suede's AI visibility work starts from the same reading.

This week, open your own listing on the public web in a logged-out browser, copy every visible word into a blank document, and read only that document. If the first two sentences do not say what the app does and who it is for, rewrite the subtitle and the opening of the description, then build the support page from the list above.
