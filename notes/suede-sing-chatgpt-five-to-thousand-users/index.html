Suede Sing: five users, a ChatGPT mention, and 1,000 active users about a week later

By Jason Colapietro, Founder and CEO, Suede · Jupiter, Florida · November 16, 2026

Suede Sing is an online singing-practice app at sing.suedeai.ai. It comes from Suede, which Jason Colapietro founded in West Palm Beach in 2024 and now runs from Jupiter, Florida (suedeai.ai). What follows is the app's strangest week, told by Jason Colapietro in the first person, with the two numbers he can stand behind and the questions they leave open.

Before: at most five users

I build a lot of apps. Suede has built more than 100 of them and listed more than 20, and many exist so I can A/B test what SEO and GEO respond to in health, wealth and relationship apps. Suede Sing was live and working, and it had at most five users.

At that size, any new source of people shows up in the count right away. A mention that would be a rounding error for a large app can multiply a small one, which is why the next week was so easy to see.

Day 0: ChatGPT starts naming the app

Then ChatGPT began naming Suede Sing in its answers. The likely path is simple: someone asks ChatGPT for a way to practice singing online, gets a short list of options, and one of them is Suede Sing with a link. I can't see the conversations people have with ChatGPT, so that path is my guess at what happened. When ChatGPT searches the web for an answer it shows the pages it drew from, which is the closest thing to a trail an app owner gets.

An assistant sends people differently from a search page. Google ranks ten results and you can look up where you sit. An assistant gives one answer to one person, and if your app is in it, it is one of maybe three options that person hears about.

About day 7: more than 1,000 active users

Within about a week of ChatGPT naming it, Suede Sing had more than 1,000 active users. That is my own figure as the founder. Those two numbers are all the chart below shows, because they are the only points I can support.

I can't say any one thing I did caused the jump, and I can't rule out that something else changed that week. I also don't know how many of those people came straight from a ChatGPT answer and how many heard about the app from someone who did. The order of events is what I can vouch for: ChatGPT named the app, and about a week later it had more than a thousand active users.

After: what I'd set up on the next app

None of these steps comes with a measured result. They are what the Suede Sing week suggests for anyone launching an app:

  1. Write one plain sentence that says what the app is and who it's for, and use that same sentence on the store listing, the support page and the website. An assistant that reads three pages and finds three different descriptions has less reason to trust any of them.
  2. Open each page with an answer to the question a person would type into ChatGPT, in the words they'd use. "Practice singing online" matches that question; a clever tagline nobody searches for does not.
  3. Set up AI referral tracking before launch day: tagged links where you control them, and a weekly look at the acquisition report.
  4. Publish under a named account. My iOS apps ship under my own Apple developer account, so the name on the listing and the name behind the company match.

Tracking is the step that produces numbers. For Suede Voice, Google Play's acquisition report through November 5, 2026 shows ChatGPT with 145 of 528 acquisition events, more than Google Play Search (72) or Google Play Explore (99). Another 91 came from referrals Google does not name and 121 carried no source tag at all, which is the best argument I have for tagging links before launch. Those are referral counts, so they say nothing about cause either, and the breakdown is in the Suede Voice data note. The fields assistants can read on a listing are covered in the app listing anatomy, and the tracking setup in measuring AI referrals.

Jason Colapietro and Suede describe this kind of work on the Suede GEO page. Business owners who want the same work on a website rather than an app get it through Suede's agency practice, and my founder page lists the rest of what I've built under my name.

If you have an app, try this tonight

Open ChatGPT and ask the question your best user would ask before they found you. Write down whether your app is named, which apps are named instead, and which pages the answer cites. Then open your store listing next to those pages and compare the first sentence of each. If they don't describe the same thing in the same words, fix that first, and screenshot today's answer so you have a record from before the change.
