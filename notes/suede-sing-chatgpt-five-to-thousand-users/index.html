Suede Sing: five users, a ChatGPT mention, and 1,000 active users about a week later

By Jason Colapietro, Founder and CEO, Suede · Jupiter, Florida · November 16, 2026

Suede Sing is an online singing-practice app at sing.suedeai.ai. It comes from Suede, which Jason Colapietro founded in West Palm Beach in 2024 and now runs from Jupiter, Florida (suedeai.ai). What follows is the app's strangest week, told by Jason Colapietro in the first person, with the two numbers he can stand behind and a plain list of what he can't claim.

Before: at most five users

I build a lot of apps. Suede has built more than 100 of them and listed more than 20, and many are there so I can see what Google and AI assistants pick up on. Suede Sing was one of the listed ones. It was live and working, and almost nobody used it.

It had at most five users. For a small app with no ad budget and no press, five users is an ordinary starting point, and I'd seen plenty of my apps sit at that level for months. I wasn't watching it closely, which matters for the next part, because it means I don't have a careful record of every change that happened around it.

Day 0: ChatGPT starts naming the app

Then ChatGPT began naming Suede Sing in its answers. The likely path is simple: someone asks ChatGPT for a way to practice singing online, gets a short list of options, and one of them is Suede Sing with a link. I can't see the conversations people have with ChatGPT, so that path is my best reading of it, not a log.

That was when I started thinking of an assistant as a distribution channel for my own products. Search engines send traffic in proportion to a ranking you can look up. An assistant gives one answer to one person, and if your app is in it, you're one of maybe three choices instead of one of ten blue links.

About day 7: more than 1,000 active users

Within about a week of ChatGPT naming it, Suede Sing had more than 1,000 active users. That is my own figure as the founder, from the app's own numbers. The jump from at most five to more than a thousand is the whole case, and the chart below has no points I can't support.

Here is what I can't tell you. I can't tell you any one thing I did caused it. I can't tell you nothing else changed that week, because I wasn't tracking the app closely enough to rule that out. I don't know how many of those people came straight from a ChatGPT answer and how many heard about the app from someone who did. What I can say is the order of events: the app was named, and about a week later it had more than a thousand active users.

That's a weaker claim than most case studies make, and I'd rather publish the weaker claim with my name on it than a strong one I'd have to walk back.

After: what I changed on the next apps

The week changed how Suede ships an app. None of these steps has a measured result attached. They're habits I now follow on every launch:

  1. I write one plain sentence that says what the app is and who it's for, and I use that same sentence on the store listing, the support page and the website. An assistant that reads three pages and finds three different descriptions has less reason to trust any of them.
  2. The first lines of each page answer the question a person would type into ChatGPT, in the words they'd use. "Practice singing online" beats a clever tagline nobody searches for.
  3. I set up AI referral tracking before launch day. Tagged links where I control them, and a habit of reading the acquisition report every week.
  4. Every app ships under my own Apple developer account, so the name on the listing, the company and the person answering support mail all match.

The third habit is the one with numbers I can show. On Suede Voice, a later app, ChatGPT is the largest named source in Google Play's acquisition report through November 5, 2026, with 145 of 528 acquisition events. Those are referral counts, so they say nothing about cause either, and the breakdown is in the Suede Voice data note. The fields assistants can read on a listing are covered in the app listing anatomy, and the tracking setup in measuring AI referrals.

Across iOS and Android, ChatGPT drove 47% of all our app downloads. The case study has the store-by-store breakdown.

For Jason Colapietro and Suede, the Suede Sing week became the reason the company treats GEO as part of shipping an app, the same way it treats a privacy policy or a crash reporter. Business owners who want the same work on a website rather than an app get it through Suede's agency practice, and my founder page lists the rest of what I've built under my name.

If you have an app, try this tonight

Open ChatGPT and ask the question your best user would ask before they found you. Write down whether your app is named, which apps are named instead, and which pages the answer cites. Then open your store listing next to those pages and compare the first sentence of each. If they don't describe the same thing in the same words, fix that first, and screenshot today's answer so you have a record from before the change.
