Turning Customer Proof into Structured Evidence: Reviews, Case Notes and Citations That Hold Up

By Jason Colapietro, Founder and CEO, Suede · West Palm Beach, Florida · November 15, 2026

"Clients love us" is a sentence anyone can type, and an AI assistant answering a buyer in West Palm Beach has no reason to repeat it. A dated review on a platform you do not control, a case note with a baseline and the client's sign-off, or an article in a publication that chose to write about you: those are statements a model can check, and checked statements are the ones that end up in answers.

Jason Colapietro's book Stake Your Claim argues that what you can prove is what you own. An earlier note on this site covered the ledger Suede keeps for its own claims. This one deals with harder material: proof that comes from customers and third parties, and how to shape it into evidence that survives a skeptical buyer, a search engine and a language model.

Three kinds of customer proof, and what each one is for

They do different jobs and break in different ways.

  • Reviews supply volume and recency. They live on platforms such as Google, Clutch or a trade marketplace, and they are credible precisely because you cannot edit them.
  • Case notes supply depth: what was broken, what was done, what changed and how it was measured. They live on your site, so their credibility depends entirely on how carefully they are written.
  • Citations supply independence: a journalist, directory or analyst describing you in their own words. They live on other people's domains. You cannot manufacture them, only earn them and organize them.

A business with only one kind looks lopsided. Hundreds of reviews and no case notes reads like a commodity. Polished case studies and no reviews reads like a portfolio built from three friendly clients.

Reviews: collect them where they count, then point to them

Ask for reviews on the platform where your buyers already look, by name, soon after the work is done. Ask everyone, not only the customers you expect to be happy. Inviting only satisfied customers is called review gating, and Google's policies prohibit it.

Then resist the urge to copy the stars onto your own pages as structured data. Since 2019 Google has not shown review star rich results for self-serving reviews, meaning reviews a business collects and publishes about itself on its Organization or LocalBusiness pages. The stronger move is a plain link to the third-party profile, plus a short quoted review with the reviewer's first name, the date and the platform it came from.

Attribution matters even when the source is a machine. Google's Web Guide, its AI results layer, answered the query "Suede labs ai review" by describing Suede Labs AI as "highly rated by users, earning a 5/5 rating from verified reviews." Suede publishes that as a capture of what Google's Web Guide said, never in its own voice, because the first framing is evidence and the second would only be a boast.

Case notes: write them like a lab report, not a brochure

A case note that holds up answers five questions in order: who the client is (named with written permission, or described accurately if anonymous), where things stood on a stated date, what was changed, what the result was and how it was measured, and what did not work. That last question is the one most agencies skip, and it is the one that makes the rest believable.

These are the rules Jason Colapietro applies before a case note goes live:

  1. Every number has a baseline, an end date and a source. "Calls up 40%" tells a reader nothing. "Calls tracked in the call platform, 90 days before versus 90 days after the March site launch" can be checked.
  2. The client approves the final text in writing, and the approval stays on file.
  3. Anonymous is fine; fictional is not. "A family-owned HVAC company in Palm Beach County" is honest. A stock photo with an invented name is not.
  4. No outcome is credited to one change when several happened at once.
  5. Corrections are dated, not made by silently rewriting the page.

Structurally, give each case note its own URL, a clear H1, a visible date, Article markup whose author points to a real person, and outbound links to anything public that supports it: the live site, the app listing, the merged pull request. The Suede developer docs team describes the same source-and-date discipline for technical pages in Writing developer docs that AI assistants quote correctly, and it transfers to case notes almost line for line.

Citations: organize what others actually published

Earned media is the proof you control least, and AI systems weigh it heavily because it is independent. You cannot invent it, but you can make it findable. Keep a press page that lists each publication, headline, date and link, and nothing that did not run.

Suede's own list is short and exact. Business Insider carried the announcement of Jason Colapietro opening the company's SEO cohort on September 16, 2026. TechBullion covered his iOS app launches, and Programming Insider ran a feature on content spam in the age of AI. The full set sits in the Suede press kit.

Directory rankings are citations too, and they need the same precision. Suede ranks among Clutch's Top 30 AI Consultants in Florida and Top 50 AI Consultants nationwide (November 2026). That sentence always stands on its own rather than being merged with another directory's ranking, and it links to its sources so a reader can check the current state instead of trusting the page.

Where structured evidence meets an AI readiness check

An outside audit can only read what you publish. The Suede Scan note on what an AI readiness score measures, and what it can't makes the point that no crawler observes your reputation directly. It reads the pages, markup and links you expose. Customer proof that sits in a sales deck or an inbox is invisible to it. Proof with a URL, a date and a named source is not.

A practical test for any West Palm Beach business: pick your three strongest proof points and ask whether a stranger could verify each one from your site in under a minute. Jason Colapietro holds Suede's own claims to the same standard: each one in the public evidence ledger links to a receipt.
