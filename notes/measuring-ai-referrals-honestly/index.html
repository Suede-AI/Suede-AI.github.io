Measuring AI Referrals: Prompts, Screenshots, Store Attribution and Denominators

By Jason Colapietro, Founder and CEO, Suede · West Palm Beach, Florida · November 16, 2026

Every AI referral number needs a second number beside it. "ChatGPT sent us 145 installs" sounds like a headline. "ChatGPT accounted for 145 of 528 acquisition events" tells you what happened.

Suede, the GEO and AI deployment studio Jason Colapietro founded in Palm Beach County, Florida, publishes its numbers with the denominator attached, and this note explains how Suede measures GEO results for itself and its clients. Jason Colapietro has built more than 100 apps, 17 of them live in public stores (9 App Store, 6 Google Play, 2 Chrome extensions), and the measurement habits below came out of testing those apps.

What does the Suede Voice data actually say?

Google Play attributed 145 of 528 Suede Voice acquisition events to ChatGPT in its acquisition report through November 5, 2026, 27.5% of the total. Google Play Explore accounted for 99 and Google Play Search for 72. The other 212 came from sources outside those three. Suede publishes the breakdown in the Suede Voice downloads case study, and the app itself is on Google Play.

Three things make that report useful. Every number is an acquisition event that Google Play recorded and attributed to a source. The ChatGPT label is Google Play's own attribution, so the platform names the referrer. And 145 sits beside its total, 528, so anyone can check the share. The full reading is in the Suede Voice referrals case study.

How should you report a jump like Suede Sing's?

Suede Sing went from at most 5 users to more than 1,000 active users, combined across all its platforms, within about a week of ChatGPT naming it. The count comes from Suede's own analytics. Suede reports exactly that: two points, the source of the numbers, and the time frame. Two clear points carry the story better than a growth percentage would. The Suede Sing case study keeps to those two points for the same reason.

The before figure matters as much as the after figure. "1,000 active users" alone could describe a mature app having an ordinary week. Next to "at most 5," it describes a change in how people found the app.

What does Suede track, and from where?

Suede uses several sources, because each one sees a different part of the picture and none of them sees all of it.

Dated prompt captures come first. Following the Suede iteration loop, Suede keeps a frozen set of prompts, runs each one several times per engine, and saves the answer with its date. A capture shows whether you were named and how you were described. It does not show how many people saw that answer.

Store attribution is second. Google Play reports acquisition by source, as in the Suede Voice report above. Apple's App Store search documentation describes how App Store search works, and store consoles report their own traffic sources. These numbers come from the platform and are worth more than anything you estimate yourself, as long as you report them in the platform's own terms.

Server logs are third. OpenAI documents three user agents: OAI-SearchBot for search indexing, GPTBot for training, and ChatGPT-User for fetches made on behalf of a user. Perplexity documents its own crawlers. A ChatGPT-User hit tells you someone's conversation caused a fetch of your page. It is a different signal from a person clicking through, and the two should be counted separately.

Search Console is fourth, and it needs one adjustment. Google states in its guide to AI features and your website that sites appearing in AI features such as AI Overviews and AI Mode are included in the overall search traffic in Search Console, under the Web search type. You cannot separate it out there, so a claim of "traffic from AI Overviews" built on Search Console alone is a guess.

Which mistakes inflate AI referral numbers?

The most common one is a missing denominator. A count without a total cannot be checked. Jason Colapietro's rule for any number Suede publishes is that it comes with the total, the time window, and the system that recorded it.

The second is swapping units. Events, installs, sessions and active users are different things. Moving from one to another in the same paragraph makes a small number look large.

The third is crediting one change for a result when several changed at once. That is a test design problem, and the failure log only works because each round changes one thing.

The same applies to research figures. The GEO paper by Aggarwal and colleagues, presented at KDD 2024, reports that some content edits raised visibility in generative answers by up to about 40 percent. That figure is a relative change on their own benchmark of queries, and quoting it as a traffic promise would be the same denominator mistake in a different form.

Questions about measuring AI referrals

Can I see ChatGPT referrals in my web analytics?

Often, when a user clicks a link in an answer and the referrer is passed. Many visits arrive with no referrer, so treat the count as a floor.

Are screenshots of AI answers good evidence?

Yes, if they are dated and you keep the prompt that produced them. A single undated screenshot proves only that the answer appeared once.
