Structured Data for Apps: Using SoftwareApplication Schema to Pin Down Identity

By Jason Colapietro, Founder and CEO, Suede · Palm Beach County, Florida · November 16, 2026

Most advice about app schema is about stars. Add SoftwareApplication markup, the thinking goes, and Google may show a rating and a price under your result. That can happen, but it is the smaller reason to do the work. For an app that wants to be named in AI answers, structured data matters because it says, in a form a machine does not have to guess at, which app this is, who made it, and where the official listings live.

Suede, the GEO and AI deployment studio Jason Colapietro founded in Palm Beach County, Florida, ships a lot of apps with similar names. Suede Sing, Suede Voice, Suede Studio Guitar, Strumly, Suede Social and Suede Scan all come from the same developer, and several of them deal with sound or the voice. An engine that blurs two of them will describe the wrong product to someone who asked a clear question. The markup is how Suede keeps them apart.

Two jobs, and the one that matters more

Google's software app structured data guide covers the rich result: it asks for a name, an offer with a price (zero is fine for a free app), and either a rating or a review, and it recommends an operating system and an application category. If your app has no reviews yet, you are not eligible for that display, and no amount of markup changes that.

The identity job has no such requirement. SoftwareApplication and its subtype MobileApplication let you state the category, the platform, the install URL, the author and, through sameAs, the other pages that describe the same app. A brand-new app with zero ratings can do all of that on day one. Jason Colapietro treats this as the default and the rich result as a bonus that arrives later, if reviews do.

What an app's graph looks like

Each app gets one node with a stable @id on the page that describes it. That node points out to a small number of things, and every pointer is a URL that already exists:

  • author and publisher reference the founder and the company by their canonical @id, rather than redefining a person or organization on every page. JSON-LD 1.1 lets one document refer to a node defined elsewhere, and that is how Suede keeps a single record of who Jason Colapietro is.
  • sameAs lists the store pages, such as the Suede Voice listing on Google Play, and for iOS apps the listing reachable from the Apple developer page.
  • applicationCategory and operatingSystem say what the app is for and where it runs, in the same words the store uses.

A trimmed version for one app looks like this:

{
  "@type": "MobileApplication",
  "@id": "https://example.com/app/#app",
  "name": "Suede Voice",
  "operatingSystem": "Android",
  "applicationCategory": "MusicApplication",
  "author": { "@id": "https://suedeai.ai/founder#person" },
  "sameAs": ["https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=ai.suedeai.suedevoice"],
  "offers": { "@type": "Offer", "price": "0", "priceCurrency": "USD" }
}

The @id in the sample uses a placeholder domain; on a real page it is the app page's own URL plus a fragment.

The rule that keeps the graph honest

Everything in the markup has to be visible on the page and true on the listing. Google's structured data guidelines, linked from its software app guide, say markup should describe content that is visible to users. A rating that sits in the JSON-LD and nowhere on the page is exactly the mismatch Suede removes before shipping.

Before a page ships, Suede checks that the name in the schema matches the name on the store listing character for character, that the category and platform match what the store shows, and that any rating in the markup also appears on the page with a source. The note on store listings as sources covers the listing side of that match.

How Suede tests a schema change

Schema is a good fit for the Suede iteration loop because it can change without changing a word of visible copy. Jason Colapietro has built more than 100 apps, 17 of them live in public stores (9 App Store, 6 Google Play, 2 Chrome extensions), and many existed to try one variable at a time. For schema, the variable might be adding sameAs links to the store pages, or replacing an inline author object with a reference to the canonical founder @id. Suede asks the same fixed prompts before and after, such as "what does Suede Voice do" and "who makes Suede Sing," and records whether the answers name the right app and the right developer.

The question is narrow on purpose: does an engine describe this specific app, and its developer, correctly? A dated capture answers that with a yes or a no, which a broad question like whether schema helps GEO never will. Questions people ask

Is MobileApplication better than SoftwareApplication?

MobileApplication is a subtype, so it inherits everything. Use it for apps that run on phones; use WebApplication for anything that runs in the browser, and give each version its own node if an app exists in both forms.

Do I need ratings in my schema?

Only for Google's rich result. For identity, leave ratings out until they exist on the page with a source.
