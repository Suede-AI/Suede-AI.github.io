Those five rows add up to the whole report. ChatGPT accounts for 145 of the 528 events, about 27%, which is more than Google Play Search (72) or Google Play Explore (99) brought in on their own. The figures come from the acquisition report for Suede Voice, an Android app from Suede, the company Jason Colapietro founded in 2024 in Palm Beach County, Florida (suedeai.ai). The original capture and context are on the Suede Voice case study page.

A table this short invites a quick conclusion. Three things deserve a slower look first: what Google Play counts, where a ChatGPT row comes from, and what the two vague rows in the middle could be hiding.

The same numbers, drawn to scale

Drawn this way, two things stand out. ChatGPT's bar is the longest. And the two grey bars together (212 events) are larger than ChatGPT's, which is the first reason to hold the headline loosely.

What Google Play counts

The acquisition report in Google Play Console groups new users by how they reached the store listing before installing. Google Play Search means they found the app by searching inside the Play Store. Google Play Explore covers browsing: home screens, category pages, the "similar apps" rows. Everything that arrives from outside the store, through a link on a web page or in an app, counts as a referral.

The counts also depend on the date range you pick. The Suede Voice figures run through November 5, 2026, and a different range would give different numbers, so anyone quoting them should quote the end date with them.

A referral gets a name when the link carries campaign parameters. Google Play reads UTM tags on a store link, so a link with utm_source=chatgpt.com is reported under that source. ChatGPT adds that tag to many of the links it shows in answers, which is the usual way a ChatGPT row ends up in a report like this one. Links you share yourself only get a name if you tag them before they go out.

What it can't tell you

The 121 events with no source tag are people who reached the listing from outside the store through a link nobody tagged. Some will be ChatGPT links copied and pasted without the tag, links opened in another assistant, shares in a messaging app, or a browser that dropped the parameters. Nobody can split them. The 91 other referrals came from sources the report doesn't name in this view, so they can't be checked one by one either.

That makes 145 a floor for ChatGPT. It can undercount in a second way too: someone might hear of Suede Voice in a ChatGPT answer, then search the Play Store by name, and count as Google Play Search. The report credits the last step it can observe, and that's the only step it knows about.

It also counts acquisitions, not the conversations behind them. There's no way to see which question was asked, what else the answer recommended, or how often ChatGPT showed the app without anyone tapping it. Comparing two periods of this report tells you a share moved. It doesn't tell you why.

Why Suede publishes it anyway

Most claims about AI referrals are screenshots of a traffic spike with no source and no denominator. This one has both. Jason Colapietro publishes the full breakdown, untagged rows included, because a reader who sees all 528 events can judge the 145 for themselves. The Suede Sing story in five users to more than 1,000 is founder-stated with no report behind it; this one has the report.

None of this needs special tooling. The acquisition report is built into Play Console, and reading it weekly takes a few minutes. The field guide to measuring AI referrals covers GA4, Play Console, App Store Connect and server logs side by side, and the note on app listings goes through which listing fields an assistant can read.

Jason Colapietro watches the 212 events nobody can attribute more closely than the ChatGPT row. Any app owner should try to shrink that group before making decisions from this report, because every untagged install is one you can't learn from. Suede's AI visibility work starts from the same habit, and the agency practice applies it to client websites. More on the person behind both is on Jason Colapietro's founder page.

Checking your own report

In Play Console, open your app's acquisition report, set the date range to the last 90 days, and break the results down by traffic source. Write down four numbers: Play Search, Play Explore, any source named chatgpt.com, and the untagged referrals. If the untagged row is larger than the named ones, add UTM tags to every store link you control this week, starting with the links on your own website and support page.
