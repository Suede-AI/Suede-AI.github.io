The Monday Brand Watch: How Suede Checks What AI Answers Say About It

By Jason Colapietro, Founder and CEO, Suede · West Palm Beach, Florida · November 16, 2026

The routine produces one row per query per surface, every Monday, in a spreadsheet that nobody else reads until something goes wrong. Jason Colapietro keeps it for Suede, a Palm Beach County, Florida, business he launched in 2024 (on the web at suedeai.ai), because AI answers change without notice and the change is invisible unless someone writes down what the answer said last week. The log is deliberately plain, so that a drop stands out against weeks of identical rows.

Which queries go in?

The list is short and stays the same from week to week, so that rows can be compared. For Suede it is the bare name, the name with each service the company sells, and the founder's name:

suede
suede ai
suede geo
suede aeo
suede ai visibility
suede ai deployment
suede ai integration
suede ai engineering
jason colapietro
who founded suede ai

A business adapting this should use the phrases a buyer would actually type, plus one question in plain language. Adding queries is fine. Swapping them out every few weeks breaks the comparison, so a new query starts its own history from the week it is added.

The founder's name is on the list because some people who hear about a company search for the person who runs it. If the answer for "jason colapietro" stops mentioning Suede, or describes him without the company, the two records are drifting apart, and Jason Colapietro logs that with the same weight as a miss on the company name.

Which surfaces?

Five: Google's ordinary results, Google's AI Overview when one appears, ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini. Each gets the same conditions every time. Google runs in a private window, signed out. The chat assistants run in a new conversation with memory and custom instructions off, and with web search on where the product offers it, since that is the mode most people use for questions about companies. Location affects Google results, so it helps to note where the check was run. For Jason Colapietro that is usually Jupiter.

Answers vary from one run to the next even under the same conditions. One run per cell is enough for a weekly log, as long as nobody treats a single week as a trend.

What goes in the log?

The log has eight columns. The row under the headers is a placeholder example of an entry, not a recorded result.

Week ofQuerySurfaceNamed?PositionFacts right?Cited URLNotes
Example: [date][query][surface][yes / no][first / later / absent][yes / no, which fact][URL or none][anything odd]

"Named?" means the answer mentions the company at all. "Position" records whether it is the first thing the answer talks about. "Facts right?" checks the identity facts against the official page: founding year, founder, location, domain, and what the company does. "Cited URL" records the page the answer linked, if any, because a wrong fact usually traces back to the page it was cited from.

What counts as a drop?

A single week of absence is noise until it repeats. The log flags a row when one of these happens:

The company was named last week and is absent this week, two weeks running

One missing week gets a note. The second consecutive miss on the same query and surface gets a flag.

A fact is wrong

A wrong founding year, founder, place or domain is flagged the first time it appears. Errors spread when other systems pick them up, so these do not wait for a second week.

The cited page changed

If an answer that used to cite suedeai.ai now cites a directory or an old profile, the text may still be right, but the source is one Suede controls less. That gets a flag too.

What happens on a drop?

A flag on Monday gets worked the same day, in this order, before Jason Colapietro touches anything else on the list:

  1. Rerun the query on the same surface in a fresh session to confirm it.
  2. Open every page the answer cited and find the sentence the error came from.
  3. Check whether anything on Suede's own pages changed that week: homepage title, the one-line description, structured data, a redirect.
  4. Fix the source Suede controls first. If the error comes from a third-party profile, correct it there with the same one-liner used everywhere else.
  5. Write the fix and its date in the Notes column, then wait for next Monday's row.

The fix is almost always on a page, rarely in the answer itself. How Google assembles an overview for a brand name explains which pages feed Google's version, and the prompt-panel worksheet extends the same idea to the questions a buyer asks before they know your name. Traffic that does arrive from these assistants is a separate measurement, covered in the note on measuring AI referrals.

The routine carries over to any business with a name and a few services to check.
