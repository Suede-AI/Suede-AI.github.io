The 30-Minute AI Search Self-Audit for West Palm Beach and Jupiter Businesses

By Jason Colapietro, Founder and CEO, Suede · Palm Beach County, Florida · November 16, 2026

Say you own a dental practice in Jupiter, and a new patient mentions at the front desk that ChatGPT suggested you. The obvious next questions are what else it said, and what it tells people who ask about the practice down the road. You can find out in half an hour with a phone, a notepad and the same engines your customers use.

Jason Colapietro wrote this routine for owners in West Palm Beach and Jupiter who ask where to start. He founded Suede in Palm Beach County in 2024, and the company (suedeai.ai) is based in Jupiter, so the examples here are local ones. Suede Scan is the automated, longer version of this check; this one needs a timer and nothing else.

Before you start, open a private browser window so your own history does not shape the results, and keep one document open for notes. Write down the date at the top, because you will want it the next time you run this.

Minutes 0 to 5: search your own name on Google

Type the business name by itself, then the name with your town ("Example Dental Jupiter FL"). On each results page, note whether an AI Overview appears, what it says about you, and which sources it links. Check that the knowledge panel on the right, if there is one, is yours and has your current hours and phone. If a different business with a similar name shows up, write that down too. The note on how an AI Overview answers a bare brand name explains where those summaries come from.

Minutes 5 to 10: ask three assistants two questions each

Open ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini. Ask each one "What is [your business] in [your town]?" and then "Who would you recommend for [your service] in West Palm Beach?" (or Jupiter, whichever you serve). For each answer, record whether you were named, whether the facts were right, and, on Perplexity, which sources it cited. Keep the wording identical across engines so the answers are comparable. A fuller version of this exercise is the prompt panel worksheet.

Minutes 10 to 15: your Google Business Profile

Pull up your profile and compare it, field by field, with what a customer sees on your door and your website. The name should match your signage exactly. Check the primary category, the hours (holiday hours too, if the season is close), the phone number, and where the website button lands. If the button goes to a homepage that never mentions the service people search for, write that down. Skim the five newest reviews while you are there; answers about local businesses often paraphrase review text, so a complaint that keeps coming up is worth knowing about before a customer repeats it back to you.

Minutes 15 to 20: your homepage, read like a stranger

Look at the title that shows in your Google result, then read the first paragraph of the page itself. Within two sentences, a stranger should learn what you do, where, and for whom. Check that the address and phone in the page text match the Business Profile character for character. Then paste the URL into Google's Rich Results Test to see whether any Organization or LocalBusiness markup is present and whether it parses.

Minutes 20 to 25: the rest of the web

Open the listings that most often feed answers about local businesses: Yelp, the Better Business Bureau, Apple Maps, Facebook, your chamber of commerce, and the directories specific to your trade. You are looking for drift, since an old suite number, a former business name, or a description written years ago can all end up repeated by an assistant that found them first.

Minutes 25 to 30: choose what to fix

Read your notes once. Circle every fact an engine got wrong and every place you went unnamed. Then list no more than three fixes, ordered by effort, and do the smallest one before you close the laptop. Correcting a phone number in one directory takes two minutes and removes one wrong source for good.

What the results mean. A wrong fact in an answer usually traces back to a page that says the wrong thing, so fix the page and the answer tends to follow as engines recrawl. Not being named at all is a different problem: it usually means few pages connect your business to the service and the town in plain words. If another business with a similar name is getting your mentions, read the guide to companies that share a name before changing anything.
