Suede Sing: From at Most 5 Users to 1,000+ Across All Platforms in About a Week

By Jason Colapietro, Founder, Suede · Palm Beach County, Florida · November 16, 2026

Suede Sing is a vocal coach, a pitch trainer and a vocal range test in one small app, on the web at sing.suedeai.ai and as Suede Sing for Chrome (extension). For most of its life it had at most 5 users. Then ChatGPT started naming it when people asked for singing tools, and within about a week it passed 1,000 active users, combined across all its platforms. Both numbers come from the app's own analytics.

Suede, the GEO and AI deployment studio Jason Colapietro founded in Palm Beach County, Florida, has run a lot of experiments on its own apps. This is the cleanest result any of them has produced. This note covers what happened, what the app looked like when an AI assistant picked it up, and what Suede now builds into every launch because of it.

What was Suede Sing before that week?

It was one of the more than 100 apps Jason Colapietro has built. 17 are live in public stores: 9 on the Apple App Store, 6 on Google Play and 2 Chrome Web Store extensions. Many exist for one reason: to find out what search engines and generative engines respond to. He calls the method the Suede iteration loop. You change one thing in an app, its listing or its page, watch what moves, and carry the result forward. Across thousands of iterations in health, wealth and relationship apps, most rounds teach something small.

Sing was a single-purpose tool. You open it, it listens, and it tells you whether you hit the note and where your range sits. A handful of people had found it.

What changed?

ChatGPT began recommending it. OpenAI does not tell a site owner when its assistant starts naming them, so the first sign was the curve in the analytics. The user count went from a number you could count on one hand to four figures in roughly seven days.

The chart shows the two numbers that tell the story: the before state and where the count stood a week later.

Why could an assistant name it at all?

Jason Colapietro has watched many apps get picked up or passed over, and Sing had four things going for it.

  • The page says what the tool does in the words people ask with. Someone types "free vocal range test" or "app to check if I'm singing in tune", and the page describes exactly that.
  • The app does one job, so a model summarizing it has little room to get it wrong.
  • The URL is stable and public, and the tool runs in the browser, so a recommendation sends the user somewhere that does what the answer promised.
  • ChatGPT's search features fetch pages through crawlers OpenAI documents, including OAI-SearchBot and ChatGPT-User. Suede checks that its apps do not block them by accident in robots.txt.

None of that is exotic. The research points the same direction: the GEO paper from Aggarwal and colleagues (KDD 2024) found that adding citations, quotations and statistics to a source raised its visibility in generative answers by up to about 40 percent in their tests.

What does the jump show?

It shows that a generative answer can move a small product from nearly zero to real usage fast.

The store data points the same way. Across iOS and Android, ChatGPT drove 47% of all Suede app downloads, and Google Play attributed 145 of 528 Suede Voice acquisition events to ChatGPT, more than it attributed to Play Search or Explore. The note on Suede Voice's Google Play data walks through that report source by source.

Jason Colapietro uses the Sing result as a blueprint. Each element of Sing's page goes into other apps one variable at a time, and the elements that repeat become rules for client work. That is how the app lab turns a breakthrough into a method clients can use.

What should a small product owner take from it?

Start by writing your main page so an assistant could quote it without editing: what the product does, for whom, and what happens when someone opens it. Keep that description identical in your store listing, your site and your structured data. Make sure the crawlers that feed AI answers can reach the page. Then record a dated before figure, so the change is clear the moment it comes. Suede now runs the launch checklist every Suede app now ships with.

Questions people ask about the Suede Sing jump

How fast did Suede Sing grow?

From at most 5 users to more than 1,000 active users, combined across all its platforms, within about a week of ChatGPT starting to name it, in Suede's analytics.

Who are the 1,000 active users?

People using Suede Sing, combined across all its platforms, including the web app at sing.suedeai.ai and the Chrome extension, counted in Suede's own analytics.

Can this be repeated for a client?

Yes. Suede's client work at suedeai.agency applies what the app lab found: an answer-first page, the same facts on every profile, open crawler access and a dated baseline, so the gain is measurable from the first week. The record behind it is on Jason Colapietro's founder page.
