GEO Is Not SEO With a New Name: The Places They Split in Suede's App Tests

By Jason Colapietro, Founder and CEO, Suede · Palm Beach County, Florida · November 16, 2026

A popular line in marketing this year says GEO is just SEO with a new acronym. Do good SEO, the argument goes, and AI answers will follow. Part of that is true. Pages that cannot be crawled will not be cited, and thin content does badly with both. Google itself says, in its guidance on AI features and your website, that there are no extra requirements for appearing in AI Overviews beyond its normal search practices.

Suede, the GEO and AI deployment studio Jason Colapietro founded in Palm Beach County, Florida, has a reason to doubt the rest of that argument. Jason Colapietro has built more than 100 apps, over 20 of them in public stores, and many existed to test what search engines and AI engines respond to. Across thousands of app iterations, the two did not always reward the same things. This note lists the specific places they split.

Split one: a ranking is a list, an answer is a shortlist

A search results page shows ten links and a person picks one. Position four still gets some traffic. An AI answer usually names a handful of options, sometimes one, and the person often acts on the first name they hear, so nothing like position four exists.

Suede Sing showed what that means in practice. The vocal coach and pitch trainer had at most 5 users. After ChatGPT began naming it, it passed 1,000 active users, combined across all its platforms, within about a week. One ChatGPT answer beat page one covers why a shortlist behaves this way.

Split two: the traffic arrives through a different door

Suede Voice gave Suede a cleaner data point, because Google Play reports where installs came from. In its report through November 5, 2026, Google Play attributed 145 of 528 Suede Voice acquisition events (27.5%) to ChatGPT. Play's own Explore surface accounted for 99, and Play Search for 72.

The lesson is direct: a chatbot outside the store sent about twice as many acquisition events as the store's own search box. An SEO plan built around store keywords would have been tuning the smaller door. What Google Play's attribution shows walks through every source.

Split three: engines quote other people's pages about you

Classic SEO is mostly about your own pages ranking. In Suede's tests, AI answers about an app regularly drew on pages Suede did not write in full: store listings, directory entries, the Apple developer page. That changed where effort went. A store description written for keyword matching read badly when an engine lifted a sentence from it, so Suede began writing listings as statements an engine could repeat, as covered in store listings as citation sources.

Split four: specifics and sources do more work

The GEO paper by Aggarwal and colleagues (KDD 2024) tested edits to source content and found that adding citations, quotations and statistics raised visibility in generative answers by up to about 40 percent on their benchmark, while keyword stuffing did poorly. That matched what Jason Colapietro saw in the app lab. A clear factual sentence with a source behind it travelled into answers more often than a polished, keyword-heavy paragraph.

On the basics, the two agree. Google's helpful content guidance applies to both, and so do its spam policies, which is why mass-produced pages fail in search and in answers alike.

Split five: you cannot rank-track an answer

SEO has decades of tooling built around a stable ranking you can check. An AI answer changes between runs, between engines and between phrasings of the same question. Suede measures with a fixed set of prompts, repeated runs and dated captures, the method described in the Suede iteration loop. Referral numbers, where a platform reports them, come second, and measuring AI referrals explains why the denominator matters.

So what carries over?

Crawl access, accurate content, fast pages and clean structured data carry over, and a team that skips them will struggle in both. What does not carry over is the target. SEO aims for a position on a list. GEO aims to be one of the few names an engine is willing to say, in a sentence it can repeat and check.

Questions people ask

Should I stop doing SEO? No. Google's AI features draw on the same index, and the crawl and content work serves both.

Is GEO only about ChatGPT? Suede tests across several engines. ChatGPT is where Suede has the clearest attributed data, through Google Play.
