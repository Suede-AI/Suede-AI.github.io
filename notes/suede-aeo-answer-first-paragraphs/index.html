Answer-first paragraphs: three before-and-after rewrites for Suede AEO

By Jason Colapietro, Founder and CEO, Suede · West Palm Beach, Florida · November 16, 2026

An answer engine reads the first few sentences under a heading and decides whether they answer the question someone asked. When they do, it can lift them almost word for word. When they are a welcome mat ("For over a decade, our family has been proud to serve..."), it moves on to a page that gets to the point.

Jason Colapietro started Suede in Palm Beach County, Florida, in 2024, and the company's site is suedeai.ai. The writing rule behind an answer-first paragraph is short: put the answer in the first 40 to 60 words, follow it with evidence, and link to where the reader can check it. The three businesses below are hypothetical and their names are placeholders; the Suede AEO page covers the rest of the work around the paragraph.

Rewrite one: a price question on an HVAC site

Say a heating and cooling company in West Palm Beach, call it Example Air, has a page titled "AC replacement cost in West Palm Beach." People ask assistants that exact question all summer.

Before

At Example Air, we know your comfort is our top priority. With years of experience serving South Florida families, our team of certified technicians is ready to help with all of your heating and cooling needs. Contact us today to learn more about our services and pricing options!

After

The cost of replacing a central AC system in West Palm Beach depends mostly on the unit's size in tons and on whether the existing ductwork can stay. Example Air gives every customer a written quote after a free measurement at the house, and the quote names the equipment model so you can compare it with other bids. Our pricing page lists last season's price ranges by system size.

The old paragraph never mentions cost, so an engine has nothing in it to quote for a cost question. The new one repeats the searcher's own terms (AC replacement, West Palm Beach) in its first sentence and names what drives the price. A written quote that names the equipment model is something a reader can hold the business to. The last sentence points to the page with the actual ranges.

Rewrite two: a yes-or-no product question

A hypothetical note-taking app, ExampleNotes, gets asked whether it works offline. Its help center answers like this.

Before

Great question! ExampleNotes is designed with flexibility in mind, so you can stay productive wherever life takes you. Our team is always working on new features to make your experience even better.

After

Yes, with one limit. Any note you have opened before is stored on your phone and can be edited with no connection; your changes sync when you are back online. Searching notes you have never opened needs a connection. The offline guide in our help center lists every screen that works without signal.

Yes-or-no questions reward a yes or a no in the first word, and the limit belongs right after it: an assistant that quotes the new version passes the condition along instead of guessing at it. The before version leaves a model to fill the gap from forum posts or old reviews, and that is how wrong answers about a product start circulating.

Rewrite three: the "who runs this firm" paragraph

About pages are where entity questions get answered or dodged. Take a hypothetical estate planning practice in Jupiter, Example Law.

Before

Founded on the principles of integrity and excellence, our firm brings a passion for service to every family we work with. We believe in building lasting relationships based on trust, and we look forward to helping you plan for the future.

After

Example Law is an estate planning practice in Jupiter, Florida, founded by attorney Jane Example, who meets every client herself. The firm drafts wills, revocable trusts and powers of attorney; it does not handle litigation. Each attorney's profile links to their Florida Bar listing.

The rewrite reads like a directory entry on purpose. Who, where, which services and which services it turns away are the facts an engine uses to decide that this paragraph belongs to this firm and to no other business with a similar name. The Bar link is a record the firm does not control, which makes it more believable than anything the firm says about itself. If your business shares its name with others, Jason Colapietro's note on companies that share a name covers the other signals that keep engines from mixing you up, and retrieval, entity and answer explains where a paragraph like this fits in the larger chain.

What one of Suede's apps suggests

Suede Sing, the singing-practice app at sing.suedeai.ai, went from five or fewer users to more than 1,000 active users within about a week of ChatGPT naming it, by Jason Colapietro's count. He does not credit any one paragraph or technique for that, and nobody outside OpenAI can say which sentences the model read. The useful part is smaller. When an assistant names a business, it describes it in a sentence or two, so the clearest sentence on your own pages should be the obvious one to borrow. A paragraph that is already short and checkable also leaves the engine less room to get you wrong when it squeezes your page into a reply.

Try it on one page this week

Pick the service page that brings you the most calls. Under each heading, copy the first 60 words into a blank document and read them as if they were the only thing a stranger would ever see. Rewrite any block that doesn't answer its heading, add the one fact a customer could verify, and link to the page that proves it. Then run the page through Suede Scan to see how readable it is to AI systems.
